# Quotation Module

## Estimate versus customer-facing quote

The estimate is the SyteLine working record. The quotation is the proposal communicated to the prospect or customer, often through the Estimate Response Form Report. Infor's CRM example links an opportunity to an estimate, adds Estimate Lines, and prints the Estimate Response Form Report with Print Price selected. A sent quote is not evidence of acceptance or a customer order.

**Section Summary:** Estimate is the record; quote is the customer communication based on it.

### Keywords
quotation, quote, Estimate Response Form Report, proposal

## Quote preparation and issue checklist

1. Verify prospect/customer identity, quote contact, estimate number, quote date, and expiration date.
2. Check every line's description, quantity, U/M, unit price, discount, tax, and promised or requested timing.
3. Confirm payment terms, freight, currency, and any configuration or customer-specific conditions in the estimate.
4. Produce the Estimate Response Form Report; confirm whether price should print, then inspect the output before sending.
5. Record the communication and next follow-up in the relevant interaction/task form; update estimate status to Quoted as appropriate.
6. When the customer accepts, verify any changed quantities, price, date, and terms before creating the order.

**Section Summary:** Check the customer document and record acceptance separately from quote issuance.

### Keywords
print quote, quote expiration, customer acceptance, quote follow-up

## Revisions, expiration, and nonacceptance

If a quote changes, review whether the site's process creates a revised estimate or edits the existing one; preserve the customer-facing version and approval trail under local policy. If an estimate expires, review the date and revalidate price, availability, and terms before reissuing. If the opportunity is lost, record the outcome and reason on Opportunities. The exact revision and approval workflow is **[NEEDS SYTELINE CONFIRMATION]**.

**Section Summary:** Revisions and expiration need a documented local control; quote status alone does not book an order.

### Keywords
revise quote, expired quote, lost opportunity, quote approval

## Information the customer-facing quotation should convey

A useful quote identifies the seller and intended prospect or customer, quote reference, issue and expiration dates, contact, proposed items or services, quantities and units, unit and total prices when price is meant to print, applicable discounts and tax, delivery assumptions, and payment terms. The estimate record supplies much of this information; the printed or distributed report is the document the customer receives. Review the output for omissions or formatting before sending. Exact document layout and required legal text are site-specific.

**Section Summary:** A correct estimate is necessary, and the actual issued document needs its own check.

### Keywords
quotation contents, Estimate Response Form Report, print price, validity

## Accept, revise, or decline a quote

If accepted without changes, record the acceptance and prepare the customer/order handoff. If the customer requests different quantity, price, date, ship-to, or terms, update the estimate or create a controlled revision under local procedure and issue a new report. If declined, capture the reason on the opportunity and preserve the proposal record for analysis. Before copying an accepted estimate into an order, check that the quote has not expired and that prices, taxes, delivery dates, and customer details remain valid. The new order is a separate transaction and must be verified.

**Section Summary:** Customer response determines a distinct acceptance, revision, or loss path.

### Keywords
quote accepted, quote revision, quote declined, copy estimate
